Feedback report

Sarah Chen · This House Would ban advertising targeted at children · WSDC · 1st Proposition · 7:42 / 8:00

Matter (content & analysis)

Analytical strengths

1. Strong instinct for the core issue

You correctly identified that children are impressionable and that companies have profit-driven incentives. You've touched on something really important here—the tension between child psychology and commercial interests. This is exactly the kind of tension that makes a great debate motion.

2. Recognized the business motivation

Good identification of corporate profit motives. Spotting the “why” behind advertising is a great analytical move. This shows you're thinking critically about incentives, not just surface-level arguments.

Areas to improve

1. No impactful opening hook

You started with: “The motion is THW ban advertising targeted at children.” This tells us what the debate is about, but doesn't grab our attention.

How to fix it: Imagine you're painting a picture with your opening line. Try: Every day, 10,000 children in Hong Kong see advertisements for sugary drinks—drinks that bring them a step closer to health issues with every sip. Why are we letting corporations profit from this?
2. Missing signposting

At 0:45, you transitioned directly into your first argument without telling us where we're going.

How to fix it: For a 1st Proposition speech: Today, I'll present three arguments. First, I'll explain how advertising exploits children's developing brains. Second, I'll show why this creates harmful consumption habits. And third, I'll argue why the government is the right body to solve this problem.
3. Arguments are unnamed

At 1:32, you began discussing the impact of ads on children but never gave the argument a clear name or thesis.

How to fix it: My first argument is Exploitation of Vulnerability. Children lack the cognitive ability to critically evaluate advertising, making them uniquely susceptible to manipulation.
4. Explanations need development

You said children are easy to influence but didn't fully explain why this is true, or why it matters.

How to fix it: Show the mechanism: children under 8 cannot distinguish advertising from entertainment. Then connect: they pester parents, parents give in, children consume harmful products. That is the chain of harm.
5. Underdeveloped company incentives

You mentioned that companies care about profit—correct—but you stopped there.

How to fix it: For them, a child is not a person—it's a market. We are choosing profit over childhood health, and that's the calculus we need to reverse.
6. Repetitive without depth

Between 2:15–3:00, you repeated that advertising is bad for children at least four times without new evidence.

How to fix it: Layer 1: ads target children. Layer 2: children are vulnerable. Layer 3: poor health choices. Layer 4: long-term public health costs. Layer 5: the government should prevent this.

Manner (delivery & style)

Analytical strengths

Vocal effort. You have a confident speaking voice—you can hear the passion when you talk about the subject.

Natural tone. When you speak without reading, your tone is warm and engaging. This is when you're at your best.

Areas to improve

Reading from notes at 1:15, 2:45, and 4:20. Pace became robotic. Put notes on the desk, step away, and speak to the back of the room.

Repetitive language. “The government will ban” appeared seven times in three minutes. Vary: we propose a ban / we need to end this practice / we must stop corporations.

Monotonous pacing between 2:00–3:30. Slow down and pause before a big point; raise volume on the central conclusion.

No gestures / physical engagement. Stand up. Use your hands. It bleeds into vocal energy.

Eye contact (audio cue) at 4:10 — a drop in certainty. Look up every 10–15 seconds.
